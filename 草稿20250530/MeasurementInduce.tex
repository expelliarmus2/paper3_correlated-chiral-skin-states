This state differs from the localized state induced by the interplay between non-Hermiticity and many body interaction.

	Non-Hermitian phenomena have attracted significant attention for their unique properties. Notably, theoretical studies based on lattice models, such as the non-Hermitian Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) model, reveal exotic bulk-boundary correspondences distinct from their Hermitian counterparts~\cite{RN1166,RN1168}. 
	A hallmark of non-Hermitian physics is the skin effect, where an extensive number of eigenstates are exponentially localized at the system's boundary under open boundary conditions~\cite{gong2018topological,kawabata2019symmetry,RN1212,RN1214,RN1217,RN1160}. %These models are experimentally realizable in platforms including photonic waveguides~\cite{nie2023non}, photonic~\cite{RN1165,RN1167,bandres2018topological}, topolectric~\cite{zou2021observation,RN2,RN1225}  and optomechanic~\cite{RN632,RN1226}, where controlled nonreciprocity and dissipative effects can be engineered. 
	More than theoretic studies, non-Hermitian physics has been realized based on the mathematical equivalence between the one-body Schr$\ddot{\mathrm{o}}$dinger equation and a set of wave equations or linear equations in classical systems~\cite{RN1161}, including photonic waveguides~\cite{nie2023non}, photonic~\cite{RN1165,RN1167,RN1227}, topolectric~\cite{zou2021observation,RN2,RN1225} and optomechanic~\cite{RN632,RN1226}.

	Beyond single-particle physics, recent efforts have extended non-Hermitian skin effect to interacting many-body systems. %  MBL应该是啥？引用那些文献？后面暂时还没改。~\cite{mak2024statics,hamazaki2019non}

	We propose the scheme to realize the evolution of gain/loss non-Hermitian Hamiltonian in the MQCs, which can be realized through the circuits, as depicted in Fig.~\ref{fig:01} (a).
	The general form of such Hamiltonians contains both Hermitian and non-Hermitian components:
	\begin{equation}
		\hat{H}_{\text{non}} = \hat{H}_0 +\hat{H}_{\text{im}}. \label{eq:non-hermitian}
	\end{equation}
	% The Hermitian part $\hat{H}_0$ is the sum of onsite energy $\hat{c}_i^{\dagger}\hat{c}_i$, hopping term $\hat{c}_i^{\dagger}\hat{c}_j$, and the interaction term $\hat{n}_i\hat{n}_j$. 
	% The non-Hermitian part $\hat{H}_{\text{im}}$ is the sum of gain/loss energy on each site, which holds imaginary onsite energy, such as $\hat{H}_{\text{im}} = \sum_{i\in \text{dissi}} \mathrm{i}\tilde{\gamma} \hat{c}_i^{\dagger} \hat{c}_i$ for dissipative (gain) energy. 
	% 上面这段描述多余吗？
	After Jordan-Wigner transformation, the Hamiltonian of fermion system can be implemented through the MQCs, where each lattice site is encoded in a qubit. Throughout this work, the qubit state $\ket{0}$ ($\ket{1}$) corresponds to the absence (presence) of a fermion at the lattice site.
	The evolution of non-Hermitian Hamiltonian is realized by alternately implementing $\mathrm{d} t$ time evolution of Hermitian part and $\mathrm{d} t$ time evolution of non-Hermitian part. 
	The evolution of Hermitian part $\hat{H}_0$ is decomposed via Trotter expantion, and each of them are multi-qubit gates. 
	% The evolution of Hermitian part can be realized through quantum gates.

			\mathcal{PT} \hat{H} (\mathcal{PT})^{-1} \vert \phi \rangle &= - E \vert \phi \rangle\\
			\hat{H} (\mathcal{PT})^{-1} \vert \phi \rangle &= - E^* (\mathcal{PT})^{-1} \vert \phi \rangle.
		\end{split}
	\end{equation}

Consequently, the energy spectrum of the system is symmetric with respect to the line $\Re(E)=0$ in the energy plane.

	The MQCs for simulating non-Hermitian Hamiltonian we adapted in Qiskit is as Fig.~\ref{fig:04} (a). The time evolution of Hermitian part, including $\hat{H}_{\text{QWZ}}$ and many-body interaction term $\hat{H}_{\text{inter}}$, is implemented through quantum gates. 	To simulate the dynamics in quantum circuits, we first apply the Jordan-Wigner to the Hamiltonian. 
	Qubit state $\ket{1}$ represents the occupation of the site and qubit state $\ket{0}$ represents the unoccupation of the site. 
	% Throughout this work, the $A$ and $B$ sublattice site of the unit cell $(x, y)$ are arranged according to $2(y+L_y x)+1$ and $2(y+L_y x)+2$. 
	Throughout this work, we employ the following sublattice indexing convention: for each unit cell $(x,y)$, the $A$ and $B$ sublattice sites are respectively encoded as $2(y+L_y x)+1$ and $2(y+L_y x)+2$.
	The anticommutation relation of fermionic operators introduces a phase factor -1 when fermions are exchanged. To maintain this property in our qubit representation, we implement the Jordan-Wigner transformation on the Hamiltonian.
	%the indices $2(y+L_y x)+1$ and $2(y+L_y x)+2$ of basis $\ket{11000\cdots}$ denote the $A$ and $B$ sublattice site of the unit cell $(x, y)$, respectively.

This three-qubit gate is marked as $U_{AA}^x$ in Fig.\ref{fig:04}. Similarly, the time evolution of other terms in the Hermitian Hamiltonian can be implemented via quantum circuits. For example, $U_{BA}^x$ in Fig.\ref{fig:04} corresponds to the time evolution of the hopping term between sites $(1,1,B)$ and $(1,2,A)$, while $U_{AA}^y$ represents the time evolution of the hopping term between sites $(1,1,A)$ and $(2,1,A)$. The interaction terms can be realized using two-qubit gates.

To realize the gain (loss) non-Hermitian Hamiltonian dynamics, we initialize the ancillary qubit to state $\ket{1}$ ($\ket{0}$). After applying the unitary gates, we measure the ancillary qubit and store the measurement outcomes in classical registers. 
	By repeating this circuit $N$ times, we effectively simulate non-Hermitian dynamics of time $N\mathrm{d}t$.
	The unitary gates $U_M$ can be realized through CNOT gates and $R_y(\theta) = e^{-\mathrm{i} \hat{\sigma}_y \theta}$ ($\theta = \arccos(e^{-\gamma \mathrm{d}t})$) gates, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:04}(a).

	\end{equation}

	We define $\mathrm{d} t$ as the measurement interval, which is the unitary evolution time between measurements in two layers.

	$\varepsilon(\rho) = \sum_{\beta} M_{i, \beta} \rho M_{i, \beta}^{\dagger}$, and $\rho$ is density matrix of the fermionic system.

	\begin{align}

	\end{align}

	\subsection{Arnoldi method}

	Arnorldi iteration can find an approximation of the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of non-Hermitian Hamiltonian by constructing an orthogonal basis of the krylov subspace~\cite{arnoldi1951principle}. This iteration is useful in dealing with large sparse matrices. Note that the eigenstates and eigenvalues of the Hamiltonian are represented by $\{\phi_i \}$ and $\{E_i\}$.

	The eigenstate with the extremal eigenvalue can be obtained by repeatedly applying the Hamiltonian to a random initial state $\ket{\nu_0}$
	\begin{equation}
		\ket{\nu_n} = \hat{H}^n \ket{\nu_0}.
	\end{equation}
	The state $\ket{\nu_n}$ expanded with $\{\phi_i \}$ is 
	\begin{equation}
		\ket{\nu_n} = \hat{H}^n \left[ \sum_i \ket{\phi_i} \bra{\phi_i}\right] \ket{\nu_0} = \sum_i \bra{\phi_i} \ket{\nu_0} E_i^n\ket{\phi_i}
	\end{equation}
	The state with the largest absolute eigenvalue will have the largest weight after many iterations. 

	A more efficient method is to diagonalize the $\hat{H}$ in $n^{\text{th}}$ Krylov subspace $\mathcal{K}_n$, spanned by
	\begin{equation}
		\left\{ \ket{\nu_0}, \ket{\nu_1}=\hat{H}\ket{\nu_0},\cdots,\ket{\nu_n}=\hat{H}^n\ket{\nu_0}\right\}
	\end{equation}
	where $\ket{\nu_0}$ is an arbitrary normalized state. The only requirement is that $\ket{\nu_0}$ is not orthogonal to the eigenstate with largest absolute eigenvalue. 
	The Arnoldi method need to find an orthogonal basis of Krylov subspace $\mathcal{K}_n$ by Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization. % 这个时候就不用就不用处理广义本征值问题。
	The orthogonalization of $\{\nu_i\}$ is 
	\begin{equation}
		\begin{split}
			\ket{\psi_0} &= \ket{\nu_0}\\
			\ket{\psi_1} &= \frac{1}{b_1}\left(\ket{\nu_1} - \ket{\psi_0} \bra{\psi_0} \ket{\nu_1}\right)\\
			&\cdots\\
			\ket{\psi_i} &= \frac{1}{b_i}\left(\ket{\nu_i} - \sum_{j=0}^{i-1} \ket{\psi_j} \bra{\psi_j} \ket{\nu_i}\right)
		\end{split},			
	\end{equation}
	where $b_m$ is the real number to ensure $\bra{\psi_i}\ket{\psi_1}=1$. The basis $\{ \psi_i \}$ is called the Arnoldi vevtors. Let $Q_n$ denote the $m$-by-$n$ matrix formed by the Arnoldi vectors, where $m$ is dimension of the matrix $\hat{H}$. The Hessenberg matrix is defined as 
	\begin{equation}
		H_n = Q_n^{\dagger} \hat{H} Q_n.
	\end{equation}
	This Hessenberg matrix can be viewed as $\hat{H}$ in the Krylov subspace $\mathcal{K}_n$. 
	Arnoldi method is Lanczos method when the matrix $\hat{H}$ is Hermitian.
	The eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the Hessenberg $H_n$ can be computed efficiently due to the low dimension of the subspace $\mathcal{K}_n$. Select the eigenvector with largest absolute eigenvalue as $\ket{\nu_0}$, and repeat the above procedure until convergence is achieved. The eigenstate with the largest imaginary eigenvalue can be obtained by applying Arnoldi iterations to the modified Hamiltonian $\hat{H} + \mathrm{i} \epsilon \mathbb{I}$. Note that $\epsilon$ is a big real number and $\mathbb{I}$ is identity with the dimension with $\hat{H}$.

	%基于该链算符，采用上升算符$\sigma^+=(\sigma^x+\mathrm{i}\sigma^y)/2$和下降算符$\sigma^-=(\sigma^x-\mathrm{i}\sigma^y)/2$表示费米子产生湮灭算符为：
	Using this string operator $P_i$, the fermionic creation and annihilation operators can be expressed in terms of the raising operator $\hat{\sigma}^+=(\hat{\sigma}^x+\mathrm{i}\hat{\sigma}^y)/2$ and lowering operator $\hat{\sigma}^-=(\hat{\sigma}^x-\mathrm{i}\hat{\sigma}^y)/2$ as follows:
	\begin{equation}
		\begin{split}
			\hat{c}_i = P_i \otimes \hat{\sigma}^{-}\otimes (\otimes_{j=i+1}\hat{\sigma}^0_j)\\
			\hat{c}_i^{\dagger} = P_i \otimes \hat{\sigma}^{+}\otimes (\otimes_{j=i+1}\hat{\sigma}^0_j),
		\end{split}
	\end{equation}
	where $\hat{\sigma}^{x,y,z}$ represent the Pauli matrices and $\hat{\sigma}^0$ represents $2\times 2$ identity matrix.

	To implement the time evolution of the Hermitian Hamiltonian $\hat{H}_{\text{QWZ}}$ and $\hat{H}_{\text{inter}}$ with elementary quantum gates, we employ Trotter decomposition for the Hamiltonian. For instance, the time evolution of the hopping term $e^{-\mathrm{i}  (\hat{c}_{1,2,A}^{\dagger} \hat{c}_{1,1,A} +h.c.)\mathrm{d} t/2}$ can be expressed, after the Jordan-Wigner transformation, as $e^{-\mathrm{i} (\hat{\sigma}_{1,2,A} \hat{\sigma}^z_{1,1,B}\hat{\sigma}_{1,1,A}^{-} +h.c.)\mathrm{d} t /2}$, which corresponds to a three-qubit quantum gates.
	%为了通过基础的量子门实现厄米哈密顿量的演化，我们对$\hat{H}_{\text{QWZ}}$和$\hat{H}_{\text{inter}}$中的每一项进行Trotter分解来实现每一项的时间演化，如跃迁项的时间演化$e^{-\mathrm{i} \frac{1}{2} (\hat{c}_{1,2,A}^{\dagger} \hat{c}_{1,1,A} +h.c.)\mathrm{d} t}$经过Jordan-Wigner变换之后为$e^{-\mathrm{i} \frac{1}{2} (\hat{\sigma}_{1,2,A}^{+\dagger} \hat{\sigma}^z_{1,1,B}\hat{\sigma}_{1,1,A}^{-} +h.c.)\mathrm{d} t}$，这是一个三比特量子门。
	The time evolution of interaction term $ U_{\text{inter}} \hat{n}_{1, 1, A} \hat{n}_{2,1,A}$ after Jordan-Wigner transformation is $U_{\text{inter}} (\hat{\sigma}^z_{1,1,A}+1)(\hat{\sigma}^z_{2,1,A}+1)/4$. The evolution of this interaction term can be realized through two-qubit gates 
	\begin{equation}
		e^{-\mathrm{i} U_{\text{inter}} (\hat{\sigma}^z_{1,1,A}+1) (\hat{\sigma}^z_{2,1,A}+1) \mathrm{d}t/4 }. \notag
	\end{equation}

	\section{The evolution of 4 fermions in the MQC~\label{sec:appendF_4Body_Evolve}}
	Here, we show the time evolution of multi-particles in the MQCs. The comparisons between the results of the MQC and exact time-evolution are given in Fig.~\ref{fig:16}. 
	% 我们考虑一个$L_x=2,L_y=4$的二维晶格，耗散作用于$y=1$的边界上，相互作用部分为$U=1.0$的$\hat{H}_{\text{inter}}$，量子态初始的四个粒子分别位于元胞$(1,1)$和$(2,1)$的$A$格点和$B$格点。
	% 在随后的时间对各个格点测量$\langle \hat{n}_{x,y,\alpha}\rangle$，相应的结果如图Fig.~\ref{fig:16}。它们分别是通过我们的方案的MQC和精确的方法对增益非厄米多体哈密顿量的模拟结果。它们之间的吻合表明我们的方案的MQC仅通过一个辅助比特能够对在更多粒子激发的非厄米多体哈密顿量进行模拟。
	We consider a two-dimensional lattice whose size is $L_x = 2$, $L_y = 4$, and gain is applied at the unit cells of $y = 1$ boundary. The interaction term is $\hat{H}_{\text{inter}}$ with interaction strength $U = 1.0$. The initial state contains four fermions located at the $A$ and $B$ sublattices of the $(1,1)$ and $(2,1)$ unit cells. We measure the average particle number $\langle \hat{n}{x,y,\alpha} \rangle$ at different time, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:16}. 
	Both simulations of MQCs (Fig.~\ref{fig:04} (a)) and exact numerical calculations of gain non-Hermitian many body Hamiltonian yield consistent results.
	% The results are obtained from both the simulation of MQCs in Fig.~\ref{fig:04}(a) and exact numerical simulation for the evolution of gain non-Hermitian many body Hamiltonian.
	The agreement between them confirms that our method based on the MQCs with an additional ancillary qubit can faithfully simulate the evolution of non-Hermitian many-body Hamiltonians.
